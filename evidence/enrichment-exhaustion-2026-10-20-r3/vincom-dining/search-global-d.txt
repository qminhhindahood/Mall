Bangkok Street Food Dessert Guide Near Khao San Home Guides Bangkok Street Food for Dessert Lovers: Thai Sweets, Snacks, and Late-Night Treats Near Khao San Road Guide Wednesday, August 26, 2026 Bangkok Street Food for Dessert Lovers: Thai Sweets, Snacks, and Late-Night Treats Near Khao San Road Coconut ice cream, mango sticky rice, roti, and more. Our guide to Bangkok street food dessert finds near Khao San, plus where to go and how to order smart. We’re wedged into the neon tide of Khao San Road at midnight when the roti pan starts its soft hiss. A ladle of batter hits hot metal, the smell of butter and banana swirls up, and the vendor flicks a knife with practiced sanuk—fun—dicing the pancake into bite-sized squares under a snowstorm of condensed milk. Across the soi, a bell dings from a coconut ice cream cart, and the sweet rot of durian drifts from somewhere we can’t see but definitely can smell. This is why we love a bangkok street food dessert wander: it’s a sugar-fueled night safari through alleys that never sleep. We’ll eat our way around Banglamphu— Rambuttri, Phra Athit Road, Chakrabongse—then hop the river for a bowl of rubies in coconut milk that ruins us for all other desserts. Bring small bills, a loose waistband, and the willingness to point and smile. Our mission is simple: more sweets, fewer regrets. Bangkok Street Food Dessert Hits You Shouldn’t Miss Mango Sticky Rice (Khao Niao Mamuang) What it is: Silky coconut cream over warm sticky rice with ripe mango slices. Sweet, salty, fatty—the holy trinity. Where to find: Around Khao San in mango season (roughly March–June) you’ll see carts stacking fruit like trophies. Chinatown’s Yaowarat Road also has reliable vendors year-round. Price: 80–150 baht depending on season and location. Pro tip: Ask for “khao niao dam” (black ... Pandan Custard (Sangkhaya) What it is: Golden twin-stick doughnuts, crackly outside, airy within. Drag them through warm pandan custard. Where to find: Early mornings around markets and some late-night oil vats near Khao San. Price: 10–25 baht per pair; custard extra. Pro tip: If you see soy milk (nam tao hoo) nearby, pair them. For more sweet inspiration near our home base, peek at our focused dessert roundup: Bangkok Street Food Desserts Guide: Best Thai Sweets, Snacks, and After-Meal Treats Near Khao San Road. Where to Eat: Markets, Sois, and After-Hours Sugar Zones Around Khao San, Rambuttri, and Phra Athit Roti carts pop up nightly—follow the clatter of spatulas. Mango sticky rice shows up on Chakrabongse and Tanao when mangoes peak. Phra Athit Road has calmer, cafe-adjacent sweets and coconut ice cream in the evening breeze off the Chao Phraya. Chinatown ... on roadside grills. Bangkok’s sweet scene is a cultural mash: royal Thai technique, Chinese-Thai syrup arts, Muslim roti flair. That’s why a bangkok street food dessert run can taste like three countries and a temple fair in one evening. Practical Tips for Ordering (and Avoiding Sad Sweets) Follow the crowd: Turnover is freshness. We’ll queue ten minutes for a busy cart before we gamble on a lonely pan of custard. Prices: Most street desserts run 10–60 baht; mango sticky rice is the outlier at 80–150 baht. Shaved ice bowls average 30–60 baht. Ordering Thai made easy: Sawadee krub/ka (hello). “Ao an-nee” (I’ll take this one) with a point. “Mai waan maak” (not too sweet) or “waan nit noi” (a little sweet). “Mai sai kai” (no egg) if bua loy comes with one.
